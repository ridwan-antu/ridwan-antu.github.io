Research

My work moves from building mechanisms, to modelling them, to simulating them as digital twins, and to using machine learning to predict how systems behave.

Oct 2026 – Present

Hyper-Redundant Oblique Swivel Joint Manipulator

An 8-DOF hyper-redundant arm (base yaw plus seven oblique swivel joints, β = 22.5°) designed in SolidWorks. I am building a ROS 2 simulation framework (URDF/xacro, RViz) with Jacobian null-space inverse kinematics for reaching into confined spaces.

ROS 2 Digital Twin and Kinematic Analysis of the SCARA Arm

A ROS 2 model built from the prototype CAD reproduces the analytical kinematics and Jacobian to machine precision over 2,640 test configurations. The analysis shows that all singularities lie on the workspace boundary, and a link-ratio optimisation gives 63% more oriented-grasp area (0.638 to 1.040 m²).

Apr 2025 – Present

ML-Enabled Predictive Digital Twin for Smart Grid Demand Forecasting in Bangladesh

A forecasting pipeline on 10 years of hourly PGCB national demand data merged with multi-city weather records. I diagnosed a distribution-shift failure in tree-based models caused by sustained demand growth and resolved it with a trend-normalised target. SHAP quantifies how much demand history and weather each contribute. An offline scenario-simulation framework for grid decision support is in design.

Ongoing · MS thesis

Apr 2022 – Mar 2023

Ceiling-Mounted Four-Axis SCARA Robotic Arm

Integrated design project

A SCARA arm hung from the ceiling, with horizontal rail travel and a vertical lift, so it leaves the floor free. Arduino-based control, belt-pulley transmission, and 3D-printed joints with aluminium/stainless-steel parts. A DH model and Python reachability analysis show a 110.4% planar workspace gain over an equivalent fixed-base SCARA.

Apr 2023 – Mar 2024

Minimising the Sound Signature of a Quadcopter Propeller

Compared tri-toroidal and modified conventional propellers to reduce noise at comparable thrust. CFD in ANSYS Fluent with the Multiple Reference Frame (MRF) approach, validated experimentally on 3D-printed propellers. The modified conventional tri-blade showed the smallest simulation-to-experiment difference (17.25%).
